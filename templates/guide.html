What Plan Yukta does for you

A fabless company owns no factories, so its planners run the supply chain through other people's reports and spreadsheets. Plan Yukta does the monthly and weekly planning cycle in one place: what is in the line today, what customers will need, what to start at the fab and OSAT, whether the testers can cope, and what to send each supplier.

One planning cycle, five modules

1 · Material now→2 · Demand forecast→3 · Module-level supply plan→4 · Tester capacity→5 · Monthly B2B package

Every module feeds the next. Today's WIP is netted in the supply plan; the supply plan sets tester demand; both become the rolling forecasts you send to your FAB and OSAT. Fill one Excel template, upload it, and all five run together.

1 · Material now

Where is every wafer and die right now, and can I trust the OSAT report?

  • Fab WIP and fab shipments by product; late fab lots against committed dates.
  • OSAT WIP by operation after removing rows the report counts twice, with the reason for each removal.
  • Excess & obsolete stock, final-test binning, a gross-die capacity check and lot lead times.

2 · Demand forecast

How much will customers take each month?

  • Statistical forecast from your shipment history using Holt's method: exponential smoothing that tracks both the level and the trend of demand. It is named after Charles C. Holt, who published it in 1957.
  • Blend it with customer forecasts, or use either on its own.
  • Forecast accuracy (WMAPE) and bias per product, so you know how much to trust it.
  • A demand uplift lever for upside and downside scenarios.

3 · Module-level supply plan

What do I need to start, where, and when?

"Module" means each manufacturing step a fabless product passes through: fab → bump → wafer sort → assembly → final test. Each has its own yield, cycle time and supplier. The plan works backwards from demand:

  • Nets demand against finished goods, today's WIP and the die bank (sorted good die).
  • Keeps a safety stock in weeks of supply.
  • Offsets each requirement by the cycle time of every module and grosses it up by its yield.
  • Rounds wafer starts to whole lots, and flags anything that would have had to start in the past ("inside lead time").
  • Gives the monthly load on each module, which is what your FAB and OSAT need to know.

4 · Tester (ATE) capacity

Will the OSAT have enough testers?

  • Throughput per product and insertion from test time, sites, index time and, for wafer sort, touchdowns per wafer.
  • Tester hours needed each month against hours available (testers × hours × OEE).
  • Utilisation by month, flagged at 85% (tight) and 100% (overloaded), with the number of testers needed.
  • Lets you agree tester reservations or extra testers with the OSAT before the load arrives.

5 · Monthly B2B workflow with your FAB and OSAT

Most fabless teams run a monthly cycle: refresh demand, agree it, plan supply, send rolling forecasts, collect supplier commits and chase the gaps. Plan Yukta produces the forecasts and the commit sheet for that cycle.

  • Fab request: wafer starts by month.
  • OSAT request: bump, sort, assembly and final-test loading by month, plus tester hours.
  • Time fences: frozen months are firm orders, slushy months can change within agreed flex, liquid months are forecast only. You set how many months each covers.
  • Commit sheet: a downloadable workbook with REQUEST, COMMIT and GAP rows. Suppliers fill COMMIT and the gaps calculate themselves.
  • Workflow calendar: who does what on which working day. It is compatible with RosettaNet-style exchanges (forecast PIP 4A-series, WIP 7B1, shipment notice 3B2) or plain Excel and CSV.

How the plan works backwards, from demand to wafer starts

A fabless plan is built in reverse. You start from what customers need and in which month, then walk back through every module. Each step removes that module's cycle time from the date and divides by its yield, until you know how many wafers to start at the fab, and when. The finished plan then drives the tester load and the B2B forecasts.

The plan uses monthly buckets, so cycle times are rounded to whole months (1 month = 4.33 weeks). If step 6 lands before the plan's first month, the plan flags inside lead time: that demand can only be covered from stock or current WIP.

Things to try

On Play with the sample, change one lever at a time and re-run.

  • Demand uplift +40%. The final-test testers go over 100% in several months, and the alerts show how many testers you would need. Then also set Extra testers per type = 1: the overloads clear. That is the conversation to have with your OSAT.
  • Test time change +50%. Tester load rises with no change in demand, as when a new test program is longer.
  • Fab cycle time 20 weeks. Some wafer starts now fall inside lead time and shortages appear: a longer fab queue has to be covered by inventory or earlier starts.
  • Wafer sort yield 70%. More wafer starts for the same demand, and a higher load on fab and sort.
  • Forecast method: Statistical only. Compare with the customer-driven plan for the ramping product.
  • Safety stock 8 weeks. More wafers start to build the extra stock, and the first months show inside-lead-time alerts, because the extra stock cannot be built in time.
  • Capacity tolerance 1.60, or E&O bin baseline BIN3. These change the Material now checks: the over-capacity flag clears, or downgraded bins stop counting as E&O.
  • Your own data. Fill the template's Demand, Plan_Params, Tester_Params and Tester_Capacity sheets along with your WIP reports. Its Plan_Quick sheet gives a formula-only first cut in Excel.

Industry methods used

  • Time-phased MRP netting with lead-time offsetting, yield grossing, lot sizing and safety stock in weeks of supply.
  • Holt's linear trend method (exponential smoothing of level and trend); weighted MAPE and bias for forecast quality.
  • ATE throughput: units per hour from test time, index time and parallel sites; wafer-sort touchdowns with stepping efficiency; capacity at OEE.
  • Rolling forecasts with frozen, slushy and liquid time fences and a supplier commit-and-gap loop.
